01

A single source of truth for a partner program

EXCEL → DASHBOARDDATA RECONCILIATIONSTAKEHOLDER REPORTING

The problem

A healthcare partner program's reporting lived across separate workbooks: certifications in one place, client activation requests in another, subscription accounts in a third. Answering a basic program question meant manual digging, and no two answers were guaranteed to match.

What I did

I consolidated the program's records into a four-tab analytics dashboard covering certifications, activations, subscriptions, and a partner directory. Every metric drills down to the underlying records and exports to CSV, so any number on screen can be traced and taken away.

The judgment call

Before shipping, I reconciled the dashboard field by field against the source workbooks and dropped any figure the sources couldn't support. Trust was the feature: a metric nobody can trace gets ignored, and a wrong one gets remembered.

Outcome

Program questions that used to require opening three workbooks now have one answer in one place. The dashboard went through 18 versions of stakeholder feedback and reconciliation passes before I called it done.

02

Baselining support burden across 700+ vendors

DATA ANALYSISTAXONOMY DESIGNPERFORMANCE ENGINEERING

The problem

A support-reduction initiative needed a starting line. Roughly 11,000 developer-support tickets spanning 700+ vendors existed only as unstructured titles in a queue. Nobody could say where the burden actually came from, so nobody could say whether it was shrinking.

What I did

I derived a two-level classification taxonomy (12 categories, ~35 topics) from the unstructured ticket titles and applied it across the full dataset with zero uncategorized records. That made the distribution visible for the first time: a small group of vendors drove nearly half of all ticket volume, and self-service opportunities clustered in a handful of predictable categories.

The judgment call

The analysis shipped as a self-contained browser dashboard, which started at 2.3 MB and was too heavy to share. I rebuilt the data layer with dictionary encoding, column-oriented storage, and compression, cutting it to 466 KB. I used AI tools heavily for the refactor, then verified full data parity myself across every record and field before anyone saw it. Faster is worthless if it's quietly wrong.

Outcome

The initiative got measurable baselines for its key results, and leadership got a shareable view of where support effort actually goes instead of anecdotes about it.

03

An EHR data-quality dashboard, presented to leadership

UX & VISUALIZATIONALERT DESIGNTEAM OF 4 · 5 WEEKS

The problem

Healthcare data analysts working inside a client's EHR data can't easily see when data quality slips, or where. Our four-person team entered Veradigm's internal innovation challenge with a data-quality dashboard built for exactly that person, and five weeks to make it real.

What I owned

Two of the six workstreams: the interface and the alerting. The design rule for the interface was the first thirty seconds: opening the dashboard should tell you what's wrong, where, and how serious, before you click anything. The alerting side was about who hears when a metric slips, and how to route that without drowning people in noise.

The judgment call

Privacy shaped the design as much as usability: views masked by default, small cells suppressed, every data reveal written to a log. And a week before presenting, we cut every claim the shipped code didn't actually back. Our refresh ran on a daily cycle, not in real time, and saying that plainly on stage beat getting caught on it in Q&A.

Outcome

We presented to a senior leadership panel in July 2026 and took five minutes of questions tougher than any of our dry runs, which is what we had practiced for. Precise beat polished.
